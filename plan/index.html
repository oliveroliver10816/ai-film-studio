This project has failed the same way seven times. Not seven different bugs — one bug, seven costumes.

There is a second, separate pattern: judging by proxy instead of by the thing itself. One of Bob's four Adrian generations is a different man entirely, and that was only caught by putting native-resolution face crops side by side — thumbnails hid it. A success status proves the render graph ran; it does not prove the video is a video.

And a third: numbers carried across contexts. GitHub's CDN served at 2.7 MB/s and got quoted as the machine's bandwidth (HuggingFace did 34.68 MB/s minutes later — 12.8× faster). A 14B model's 205 s/video-second on an H100 got applied to our 5B on the PRO 6000, which measures 16.23.

The five rules that come out of that

  1. Every step is verified by an artifact that could only exist if the step worked — bytes on disk, a SHA-256, a duration, a pixel. Never an exit code, never a status field, never my word.
  2. All arbitrary text travels base64-encoded and is decoded on the machine. Already the rule; it stays the rule. No prompt ever goes inside a PowerShell string.
  3. Every job carries a canary — a fixed non-ASCII string echoed at the top that must return byte-identical. Silent encoding damage becomes a failed assertion instead of something spotted by eye three weeks later.
  4. Nothing is published or approved from a thumbnail, a log line, or a filename. Faces get compared as native-resolution crops. Video gets watched.
  5. Every number states where it was measured — which model, which card, which resolution, which length. A figure without its context is a guess wearing a costume.

The FLUX.2 [dev] position, precisely

The FLUX [dev] licence is non-commercial at §2(b), and §3(a) separately prohibits commercial use of "any data produced by the FLUX [dev] Model". The output grant at §2(d) is written "except as expressly prohibited herein" — so the carve-out swallows it. The images it makes are restricted, not just the model. This is different from most "non-commercial" weights and it is why it can't be quietly used for "just the look".

The route to rights is real and it is self-serve-adjacent: Black Forest Labs sells commercial licences at dashboard.bfl.ai/licensing. Their Builder tier is a self-serve purchase but covers FLUX.2 [klein], not [dev]. FLUX.2 [dev] sits in their Professional and Platform tiers, which are Contact Sales. Professional is described on their own page as "perfect for creative agencies and studios producing images on behalf of named clients" at 100K images/month across up to 3 domains. No price is published; I will not invent one.

There is also a free alternative that needs no conversation at all: FLUX.2-klein-4B is genuinely Apache-2.0 — I read its LICENSE.md and it is the real Apache text, not a lookalike — it does multi-reference editing, and it runs in 13 GB of VRAM. On a 96 GB card that is nothing.
